# Evaluation and validation

## 6. Evaluate the question, not an omnibus 'brand score'

Decide what would change the decision before collecting feedback. Show comparable specimens without revealing the intended interpretation first. Ask what they communicate; then ask reactions and reasons; preference comes later. Keep owner fit, audience understanding, visual appeal, memory, technical validity and actual behaviour separate.

A short qualitative round can reveal confusion; it cannot establish a population preference. New brands cannot pass mature-brand recall tests immediately. Check peer confusion now, and recognition after exposure. A logo-removal review is a heuristic, not proof of market ownership.

Test representative surfaces: long reading, mobile, slides and dense product content when relevant. Also examine a real touchpoint—support response, onboarding or delivery—where stated beliefs should be experienced. Never claim simulated customer reactions are actual participant evidence.

Measure actual rendered foreground/background pairs, opacity, states and supported themes. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for qualifying large text, and 3:1 for required non-text UI/graphic information against adjacent colours, subject to the standards' exceptions. Do not round failures up. Record allowed/prohibited uses; every swatch need not work on every other swatch. Do not silently repaint approved colours.

Check keyboard/focus, non-colour cues, reduced motion, text resizing/reflow, font loading, licences and asset rights. Automated checks do not establish complete accessibility. Keep technical checks separate from owner acceptance.

**Output/gate:** observed findings, measured checks, unresolved limitations and explicit acceptance. Reopen the decision responsible for a failure—not the entire project by default.
