# Discovery, comparison and definition

## 1. Discover beliefs and lived material

Establish the purpose first: personal expression, company, community or something else. Read existing work before asking people to repeat it. For an organization, founder preferences are one input—not a substitute for customers, employees and actual operations.

Ask concrete questions: What niche thing do you love? What object, ritual, edition, place or piece of work matters? Why? What do you believe enough to act on? What decision did that change? What did it cost? What would you refuse? What would feel false?

Record beliefs as **belief → evidence → decision/refusal → experienced effect → possible expression**. Separate present reality from aspiration. Preserve contradictions rather than selecting an archetype too early. A common value can matter deeply through uncommon commitment; uniqueness is not required for every belief.

Before showing model-created directions, capture the owner's unprompted associations and rough ideas. This need not be a drawing exercise.

**Output/gate:** specific source material and confirmed purpose; direct statements, observations and agent hypotheses clearly separated.

## 2. Collect references, then inspect the neighbours

Use an optional Pinterest/shared board, annotated links or the owner's own artifacts. Separate personal/cultural references, direct peers and distant analogies. Record what is liked, what must not be copied, the transferable principle, source and use rights. Liking an album does not endorse its cover.

The agent inspects a bounded set of relevant brands' actual products, language and identities—not search snippets alone. Compare audience, promise, proof, voice, type, colour, imagery and behaviour. For existing brands, audit what people may already recognize before replacing it.

Ask: Which brands should feel related? Which must we not be mistaken for? Which useful conventions should stay? How should this brand differ, and why would that difference matter? Is the difference real today or only desired? Do not invent an empty position on a decorative two-axis chart.

**Output/gate:** annotated reference collection and peer comparison; agreed relationships and confusion risks, not obligatory eccentricity.

## 3. Write the brief and observable principles

Keep it to purpose, audience/situation, point of view or promise, supporting evidence, productive tensions, exclusions and practical constraints. For audience claims, distinguish observation from founder assumption. Personal expression need not become commercial positioning.

Separate three questions: **What do we stand for? Why might someone choose us? How will they recognize us?** A visual signature cannot substantiate a business claim. An 'onlyness' sentence is an optional stress test; never publish an unsupported 'only'. Archetypes are optional creative vocabulary, not personality diagnoses.

Translate adjectives into behaviour and examples. Include voice from the start: the same message in fitting and unfitting language; different tone for celebration, failure and explanation. If naming is needed, explore territories after enough brief clarity, then screen meaning, pronunciation and availability. A domain search is not trademark clearance.

### Converge into a spoken introduction

Once the foundations are supported, optionally compress them into **five sentences or fewer** using **Vision → Gratitude → Profile → Service**. Use the [brief worksheet](../templates/04-brief-and-directions.md). This is a short expression of the brief, not a replacement for its audience, evidence, exclusions or constraints.

- **Vision:** What future do you want to help create? Name a concrete change, not a grand claim to transform everything. Phrase aspiration as aspiration, not accomplishment.
- **Gratitude:** What are you genuinely glad to work on, learn or do with others now? Ask rather than inventing gratitude; use natural language rather than compulsory thankfulness.
- **Profile:** What do you actually do that connects to that future? Prefer a specific practice or contribution over a chronological CV or title stack.
- **Service:** How can the other person join the conversation? Offer a relevant way to help or a genuine question, not an automatic sales pitch or an unsupported promise.

Default to one sentence per move, with an optional fifth only if it adds needed context. Ask where the introduction will be used; a social introduction need not become a business pitch. Draft from existing evidence, mark inferred aspirations or feelings for confirmation, and keep personal examples private until separately approved for publication.

Read it aloud. Check that a listener can tell what future matters, what the person actually does, and how to respond. If it could introduce almost anyone, sharpen the concrete change and present work rather than adding flattering adjectives. Approve the wording separately from the wider brand system.

Source: the four-part method is adapted from a user-supplied summary of an [Instagram reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DedVtVnP8hx/); the reel audio and creator attribution have not been independently verified. These prompts are our adaptation, not a transcript or a validated branding instrument.

**Output/gate:** one-page brief accepted by the owner; uncertainties and decisions that could reopen it recorded. If this exercise is used, retain a separately approved introduction of five sentences or fewer.
